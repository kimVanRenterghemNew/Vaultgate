Why Vaultgate

Your agent gets access, never the key.

An agent sends a placeholder such as {{secret:github_token}} and a virtual key of its own. Vaultgate fills in the real value for that one request, on the domain you allowed.

A prompt injection can make an agent misbehave. It cannot make it hand over a key it never had.

A simple local app, enterprise-grade security.

One install and a welcome of about two minutes: your passkeys, your first tool, done.

Underneath: libsodium encryption for everything on disk, passkeys with PRF to open the vault, a named rule behind every decision and an encrypted log of each one.

Local only, security first.

No account, no cloud, no telemetry. The vault is a set of encrypted files on your own PC.

Closed by default: without a virtual key, a grant, an allowed domain and, for anything new, your approval, a request is refused before the secret is even decrypted.

A touch, not a click.

A program can click a button. It cannot touch your passkey. Anything that lasts longer than one request asks for one.

How one request travels

The secret exists in one place: the Vaultgate process, at the moment it fills in one request. Every check before that can refuse, and its name is the answer.

  1. Who is asking?

    A valid virtual key, from an address you allowed.

    no-tool-key
  2. What, and where?

    A known secret, to its own domain, in a place it may go.

    target-host-not-allowed
  3. May it?

    A grant for this tool, secret and domain, or your answer in the tray.

    needs-approval
  4. Logged first.

    The decision goes into the encrypted log before anything is decrypted.

    audit-unavailable
  5. The real site?

    The certificate chain is checked during the handshake.

    tls-untrusted
  6. Filled in, then scrubbed.

    Only now is the secret decrypted, into this one request. An echo in the reply is removed.

    granted
# the agent knows only its own virtual key and a placeholder
curl http://127.0.0.1:8484/forward/https://api.github.com/user \
  -H "x-vault-gate-tool-key: ks_…" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer {{secret:github_token}}"

200  x-vault-gate-rule: granted

Many tools need no placeholder: point them at Vaultgate, for example with ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL, and the key goes where that service expects it.

Proof, not promises

npm run attack starts the real process with fake secrets and runs every attack in the bench. Each one ends with the rule that stopped it, or stays open with its reason.

24attacks in the bench
21stopped by a named rule
0secrets in any output

Three are open, and the security page says which and why.
